# Narrative Crossroads Solo Framework SRD
{: .no_toc }

This is the **Solo Framework SRD** for Narrative Crossroads — its **System Reference Document**. An SRD is the reusable core of a game's rules, published under a license that lets anyone build on it — take what's here, change it, and use it in your classroom or in something you make yourself.

**Solo** here is the tabletop sense, not the classroom one: each student plays alone, all at once. The class discussion afterward is the other half of the method.

This page is the canonical version. A [PDF](/downloads/Narrative_Crossroads_Framework.pdf) is available for printing, but the page is the reference — quote it, link to any section, adapt it.

A **group version** of Narrative Crossroads is in development and will be released under a different license.

## What Is and Isn't in Here
{: #scope }

The Solo Framework SRD is the **system**:

- The three lenses
- The round
- The Character Frame
- How to build your own module

The literary modules — *The Most Dangerous Game*, *The Interlopers*, *The Outsider*, *Romeo and Juliet*, the historical template — are **content built on this system**, not part of it. They live on the [Downloads]({% link downloads.md %}) page and can be replaced wholesale with your own texts without touching anything below.

## The Three Lenses
{: #lenses }

A student inhabits a character at a threshold moment and reads that moment through one of three lenses. The lenses are three ways of *reading* one moment. They are not skills, not stats, not abilities, and nothing is ever rolled against them.

| Lens | Read the moment as… | The question it asks |
|:-----|:--------------------|:---------------------|
| **Confrontation** | A clash | What are you up against, and what does facing it cost? |
| **Comprehension** | A realization | What do you understand now that you didn't a moment ago? |
| **Connection** | The bonds | Who is this really about, and what do you owe them? |

Three students at the same moment, holding different lenses, will write three different things. That difference is the point; it is what the discussion is made of.

## The Round
{: #the-round }

### 1. Same character, same moment

Everyone works from the same character at the same **threshold moment** — a point where the character could genuinely have chosen otherwise. Nobody competes, and nobody waits for a turn.

### 2. Take a lens

Students pick a lens, or roll a die to spread the room:

| Roll | Lens |
|:-----|:-----|
| **1–2** | Confrontation |
| **3–4** | Comprehension |
| **5–6** | Connection |

The die only seeds. It decides which way in you get; it never decides how anything turns out. There is nothing to succeed or fail at, so a student who dislikes their lens can simply take another.

### 3. Write in character

A few sentences, in the character's voice, at that moment, showing one thing from the evidence boxes. The writing is the play — not a record of play that happened somewhere else.

### 4. Harvest

Read several aloud, at least one from each lens. Then ask the question that does the analytical work:

> **Which reading does the text support, and what's your evidence?**

Students are not defending a score. They are defending an interpretation against the text.

## The Character Frame
{: #character-frame }

One card per student, filled in before writing. Two evidence boxes, each needing a line or page number:

| Box | Prompt |
|:-----|:-------|
| **Wants** | What does this character want right now? |
| **Fears** | What are they afraid of? |

The card also carries the character, the text, the moment, a lens strip, and space to write in character. Print it from the [Character Frame Card](/downloads/NC_Character_Frame_Card.pdf) — two to a page, half-sheets, one cut.

Evidence reflects who the character *is* in the text, not who students want them to be. That is the whole teacher move: when a student writes something vague, ask where they see it.

## Building Your Own Module
{: #build-your-own }

A module is content. To build one for any text:

1. **Select 3–5 characters**, each with textual evidence for what they want and fear
2. **Identify 2–3 threshold moments** — points where the character could genuinely have gone either way. If nobody could disagree about it, it isn't a threshold
3. **Note what the text actually does** at each moment, so the harvest has something to argue against

The three lenses do not change from text to text. They are the system; the characters and moments are yours.

Use the [Historical Template](/downloads/NC_Historical_Template.pdf) as a starting point for historical figures, or the [Modules guide]({% link practice/modules/index.md %}) for literary texts.
