# From the Workshop
{: .no_toc }

If you were in a Quest-Based Learning session, this is your follow-up page. If someone told you about it and you weren't there, everything here works without having attended — you'll just be reading the round instead of remembering it.

**If you only do one thing:** go to the [Quickstart]({% link quickstart.md %}) and run it. Everything else on this page is optional depth.

---

## What Happened in the Room

You read a short story, silently, the way a student would.

Then everyone inhabited the same character at the same **threshold moment** — the point where that character could genuinely have gone either way. You took one of three lenses; some people picked, some rolled a die for it. The die only chooses the way in. Then you wrote a few sentences in character, in that character's voice, at that moment.

Then we read several aloud — at least one from each lens — and asked:

> **Which reading does the text support, and what's your evidence?**

That harvest is the analysis. Everyone wrote at once, nobody waited for a turn, and there was nothing to win. The writing is the play.

The three claims from the session, in order:

1. **The card is the stick.** A child with a stick is a knight. A student with a character card is inside the text. The prop does the work of imagination that instruction alone can't assign.
2. **Same moment, three readings.** Everyone writes as the same character at the same threshold, each through a different lens. The disagreement that produces is what the discussion is made of — and every student arrives holding a reading to defend, because each of them wrote one.
3. **The minimum is one card, one scene, one question.** Everything else is elaboration.

## Running It Yourself

The stories used in the session are still in copyright, so the texts themselves aren't hosted here — you'll need your own classroom copies. The structure is the same whichever text you use:

- **Read it first.** Silently, in the room. The round depends on everyone holding the same scene.
- **Name the moment.** One threshold, framed out loud, for everyone at once. Pick one where a reasonable reader could argue the character should have done otherwise.
- **Hand out the lenses.** Pick or roll. What you want is all three live somewhere in the room; a die gets you there fastest.
- **Write, then harvest.** A few sentences each, then several read aloud — at least one per lens — and then the evidence question.

It scales down as well as up. One participant works; so does a staff meeting. Nobody is performing, so a small room costs you nothing.

## The Seven Procedures

The toolkit segment's premise: strip the setting off a game and keep the procedure underneath. You don't adopt a whole game — you take the procedure that does a classroom job and drop it into a text you already teach.

| Procedure | The classroom job it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Session zero** | Establishing norms students actually own |
| **Community and place** | Building shared context before content |
| **A choice that costs something** | Making stakes real enough to reason about |
| **"What do you do?"** | Converting comprehension into commitment |
| **Safety and calibration** | Giving students a way out that isn't a confession |
| **Structure that survives absence** | Running a unit when a third of the room is gone |
| **Students inventing the rules** | Handing over authorship |

The full menu — with a specific game behind each procedure — isn't posted here. Ask at <geoffrey@geoffreysperl.com> and I'll send it.
