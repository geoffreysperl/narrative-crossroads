# Implementation Guide

This guide covers the practical logistics of running Narrative Crossroads in a secondary ELA classroom.

---

## Before You Begin

### Teacher Preparation

**You don't need to be a gamer.** Narrative Crossroads is a solo journaling game played by everyone at once — students write, they don't compete. If you've never played a TTRPG, you can still run this effectively.

What helps:
- Familiarity with the text you're teaching
- Comfort with facilitation (you already do this)
- Willingness to improvise within structure

### Materials Needed

- [ ] Text being studied (students should have read relevant sections)
- [ ] Character Frame cards (one per student)
- [ ] A die per table, if you want to assign lenses by chance rather than choice
- [ ] Crossroads scenario (teacher-prepared or from [modules](modules/))
- [ ] Debrief reflection prompts

The die is optional. Students can simply pick a lens, and any online roller works if you'd rather not hand out dice.

### Time Required

| Format | Minimum Time | Recommended |
|:-------|:-------------|:------------|
| One round, quick harvest | 30 minutes | 45 minutes |
| Full session with character framing | 45 minutes | 60 minutes |
| Extended module (several rounds) | 90 minutes | Split across 2 days |

## Session Structure

### Phase 1: Frame the Character (15-20 min)

Before any writing, students gather evidence about the character they'll inhabit.

**Individual or small group work:**

1. **Identify the character** — Who are we reading today?
2. **Complete the Character Frame** — the same two evidence boxes as the printed card, each needing a line or page number:
   - **Wants** — What does this character want right now?
   - **Fears** — What are they afraid of?

**Teacher moves:**
- Circulate and push for textual evidence
- Challenge vague claims ("Where do you see that in the text?")
- Note interesting interpretations to surface in debrief

### Phase 2: Present the Crossroads (5 min)

The teacher frames the scenario:

> "We're at the moment in Chapter 7 where [character] has just learned [information]. They're standing in [location]. In front of them is [choice/obstacle]. Everyone is writing as [character], right here."

**Key elements:**
- Ground it in a specific textual moment
- Choose a moment where the character could genuinely have gone either way
- Make stakes clear
- Leave the reading open

### Phase 3: Write the Round (15-25 min)

Everyone writes at once, as the same character, at the same moment. Nobody waits for a turn.

**The round:**

1. **Take a lens.** Students pick one, or roll a die to spread the room:

| Roll | Lens | Read the moment as… |
|:-----|:-----|:--------------------|
| **1–2** | **Confrontation** | A clash. What are you up against, and what does facing it cost? |
| **3–4** | **Comprehension** | A realization. What do you understand now that you didn't a moment ago? |
| **5–6** | **Connection** | The bonds. Who is this really about, and what do you owe them? |

2. **Write in character.** A few sentences, in the character's voice, at that moment, showing one thing from the evidence boxes.
3. **Harvest.** Read several aloud, at least one from each lens.
4. **Ask the question:** *which reading does the text support, and what's your evidence?*

The die only seeds. It decides which way in a student gets; it never decides how anything turns out. There is nothing to succeed or fail at, so a student who dislikes their lens can simply take another.

**Teacher moves during writing:**
- Circulate and read over shoulders; note readings worth surfacing in the harvest
- Push for character consistency: "Would [character] see it that way? Based on what?"
- Keep the focus on the character's voice, not on plot invention

**Variations:**
- **Whole class:** Everyone writes, then harvest as one group
- **Small groups:** Harvest at tables first, then send one reading per lens to the whole class
- **Partner work:** Pairs trade papers and argue the other person's reading from the text

### Phase 4: Debrief (10-15 min)

The debrief is where learning crystallizes. Don't skip it.

**Discussion questions:**
- How did your reading compare to what the character actually does in the text?
- What did writing in character reveal that you hadn't considered before?
- What textual evidence supports or complicates your interpretation?
- How would the moment have read through a different lens?

**Written reflection options:**
- Quick-write: "One thing I understand about [character] now that I didn't before..."
- Analytical paragraph connecting roleplay to textual evidence
- Comparison: "My interpretation vs. the author's choice"

## Classroom Management

### Grouping

| Group Size | Best For |
|:-----------|:---------|
| Whole class | Teacher modeling, high-stakes moments, shared experience |
| 4-5 students | Standard play; everyone writes, the table harvests together |
| Pairs | Low-stakes practice, dialogue scenes, ML scaffolding |

### Noise Level

TTRPG-based activities are louder than silent reading. Set expectations:

- "Game voice" vs. "discussion voice"
- Signal for attention (raised hand, bell, etc.)
- Clear transitions between phases

### Student Facilitators

After modeling, students can run the harvest for small groups. Benefits:
- Develops close reading (a facilitator must know the text)
- Builds leadership and facilitation skills
- Frees teacher to circulate and assess

Train student facilitators on:
- Making sure every lens gets read aloud at least once
- Asking "what's your evidence?" and waiting
- Keeping the table focused on the text

## Assessment

### Formative Assessment During Play

Observe and note:
- Quality of textual evidence in Character Frames
- Consistency between the evidence gathered and the reading written
- Depth of reasoning when challenged

### Summative Options

| Assessment | What It Measures |
|:-----------|:-----------------|
| Character Frame worksheet | Close reading, evidence gathering |
| Debrief reflection | Analytical thinking, self-assessment |
| Analytical essay | Transfer to traditional academic writing |
| Recorded reading + commentary | Performance + metacognition |

### Connecting to Standards

Narrative Crossroads supports ELA standards including:
- Cite textual evidence to support analysis (RL.9-10.1)
- Analyze character development (RL.9-10.3)
- Participate in collaborative discussions (SL.9-10.1)

## Troubleshooting

### "Students aren't taking it seriously"

- Tighten the magic circle: clearer boundaries, explicit "we're in the character's head now"
- Increase stakes in the scenario
- Model the tone you want

### "One student dominates"

- Structured turn-taking: "Going around the table..."
- Smaller groups
- Assign roles: spokesperson, note-taker, devil's advocate

### "Students just do random things"

- Enforce the Character Frame: "Is that consistent with the evidence you wrote down?"
- Require evidence before a reading is accepted in the harvest
- Pause and redirect: "Interesting. How does the text support that reading?"

### "I don't know what to do when they go off-script"

- Improvise within the text's logic
- Ask: "What would actually happen if [character] did that?"
- It's okay to say: "Let's pause and check the text"
